# RIS Robotics Session — 2026-09-17

The session began with the robotics/communications side meeting the Radar/RIS team for the day's integration and acquisition work. Before the Jackal could be used, an operational access issue had to be resolved: the required booking system had not yet enabled the user to reserve the Jackal. The user therefore went to Alex. Because prior Husky training and operating experience already existed, the onboarding focused only on Jackal-specific differences rather than repeating general mobile-robot training. Alex demonstrated how to change the Jackal battery, how to reach and connect the experiment wiring, and the relevant USB and Ethernet connectivity. Alex then enabled the user's Jackal booking access. This was operational preparation, not a change to the system architecture.

The session then moved to the Husky work. Husky 1 had initially been booked, but the working troubleshooting and acquisition session ultimately proceeded with Husky 3. One of the Husky units examined had a metal plate covering the top/internal access area, making inspection of the computer and wiring inconvenient. Rather than spend the session locating tools to remove that plate, the work moved to the other available Husky. The existing repository evidence does not establish which exact unit carried the plate, so this record intentionally does not assign it to Husky 1 or Husky 3.

On Husky 3, the first visible symptom was that Ethernet appeared not to work. Troubleshooting moved downward through the stack and showed that Ethernet itself was not the root cause: the onboard computer was not powered. The power path was connected, but the computer still failed to start. The actual fault was incorrect/reversed polarity on the onboard computer's power feed. Once the polarity was corrected, the computer powered successfully, allowing Ethernet and higher-level interfaces to become usable. The detailed physical troubleshooting chain and the original photographs remain in [TS-001 — Husky onboard computer no power / reversed polarity](../robotics/husky/TS-001-onboard-computer-no-power-reversed-polarity.md); this session log summarizes the causal sequence rather than duplicating that incident record.

With the onboard computer operational, the session continued through the Husky Bluetooth interface and joystick setup. The PS4 controller was paired successfully and the Husky could then be driven manually. The detailed Bluetooth investigation, including the use of the existing Husky tools and the recovered pairing state, remains in [TS-002 — Husky PS4 controller Bluetooth pairing](../robotics/husky/TS-002-ps4-controller-bluetooth-pairing.md).

Once the Husky was operational and manually driveable, it was taken upstairs for the joint Radar/RIS experimental work. The Radar/RIS side collected movement data while the Husky served as a moving robot target. The acquisition examined how robot motion appeared in the sensing system and compared it with human movement, specifically looking at the **Doppler signature** and **range signature**. This was joint experimental work: the Husky supplied repeatable physical robot motion while the Radar/RIS setup captured and characterized the resulting sensing response.

A detectability problem emerged during acquisition. At the existing preferred sensing height, the robot was not detected strongly enough. The team experimentally lowered the sensing setup, which improved visibility of the robot, but the lower geometry negatively affected human detection. Because the same sensing configuration still needed to support human detection, permanently lowering the setup merely to improve robot visibility was rejected. The team returned to / retained the original preferred sensing height.

The intervention therefore moved from the sensing geometry to the target itself. Bags were placed **on top of the Husky**, not underneath it, to increase the effective physical height of the moving target and experimentally improve the robot's apparent radar visibility / apparent RCS. This is intentionally recorded as an experimental detectability intervention rather than a claim of a guaranteed or quantified electromagnetic RCS increase. It allowed the sensing setup to remain at the geometry required for human detection while making the robot target more visible to the Radar/RIS system.

The robot-movement data-acquisition campaign for this session was then completed. This conclusion applies to the specific acquisition campaign performed on 2026-09-17; it does not mean the overall RIS Robotics project is complete.

The day's work also clarified the system architecture that follows from the experiment. The Husky is not being replaced or promoted into the safety-control path. It has a distinct continuing role as the **Dummy Robot**, moving through the conflicting / hidden corridor as the obstacle observed by Radar/RIS. The Jackal has the separate role of **Controlled Robot**, moving in the controlled corridor under normal manual teleoperation through `cmd_vel` and receiving the Radar/RIS-derived safety intervention through the existing NRF/BLE and Jackal-side control path. This supersedes the earlier overall interpretation of “Husky → Jackal” while preserving the historical reasoning that Jackal is the simpler controlled platform.

The session further refined the STOP semantics. Detection of an obstacle in the conflicting corridor is not, by itself, sufficient to stop the Jackal immediately: the obstacle may clear while the Jackal is still far from the corner. The Jackal's distance to the corner must therefore gate enforcement of STOP. The Jackal still has only two logical motion-control authorities—normal teleoperation / `cmd_vel` and higher-priority safety STOP. Distance-to-corner is contextual state, not a third command. STOP becomes authoritative when the conflicting corridor is unsafe **and** the Jackal is within the configured distance threshold from the corner. The threshold remains `DISTANCE_THRESHOLD = TBD`, and the source or method used to obtain Jackal distance-to-corner remains **TBD**. No localization mechanism was selected during this session.
